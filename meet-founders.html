Meet founders

Founders, talking to other founders.

Not a pitch. Not a raise. Somewhere to find the person who already hit the wall you're standing in front of, hear thirty seconds of them in their own voice, and decide for yourself whether to say hello.

What actually happens

Two people have to agree before anything starts.

There is no inbox anyone can reach you in, and no request you have to answer. You see each other first, and either of you can end it there.

01

Your profile is thirty seconds

You record it once — what you're building and what you need right now. That is the profile. No résumé, no headline, no connection count.

02

You watch other founders

Profiles come one at a time. You can narrow the feed to founders only, and narrow it again by sector, then watch before you do anything else.

03

Nothing sends until they agree

Sending a Spark asks. They watch your thirty seconds and either Spark back or decline. No reply means no introduction — and no way to message you.

04

Three minutes, live

Once you've both said yes, you get a three-minute video conversation. It runs longer only if you both tap to keep going.

The consent rule

Asked twice, not once.

The second gate sits after the conversation, not before it.

Before

Both have to Spark

A one-sided Spark produces nothing. The pairing only exists once the other founder Sparks back.

After

Both have to say yes again

When the three minutes end you each answer privately. Chat opens only if you both said yes.

Declining

A clean no

Declining is a normal answer with a reason attached, and it stops that person Sparking you again for a while.

Enforced

Not just a screen

The rule lives in the database, not the interface. Without the mutual pairing there is no message to send.

The reason people are here

Payments are failing in a market you've never sold into before.

You've read everything there is to read. What you want is twenty minutes with someone who shipped through it eighteen months ago.

That is the whole shape of it. A hire falls through a week before they were due to start. A technical decision you made in month three turns out to be the reason nothing scales in month twenty. A country's card rails behave nothing like the documentation said they would.

None of that is a question for an investor, and none of it is a question you can post publicly with your company's name attached. It is a question for another founder — ideally one who is a year or two ahead of you, in roughly your sector, and has no stake in how you answer it.

So when you record your thirty seconds, say the actual thing. Not the polished version. The founders worth meeting are the ones who recognise the problem when they hear it, and those are the ones who Spark back.
