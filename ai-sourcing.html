The sources

Members first. Then the places technical people already publish their work.

There is no mystery database behind this. Two pools, in this order, and nobody outside them is contacted.

FIRST

FaceMeet's own members

People who have recorded a video profile and said out loud what they do and what they want next. When a role is published, the ones it matches are notified inside the app. They are already here and already reachable.

THEN

Open developer sources

For technical roles, public developer work — GitHub, Stack Exchange, Codeforces. Code someone chose to publish, answers they chose to write. It is evidence of the craft, in public, under their own name.

NEVER

People who never signalled

Someone is only approached where they have published a way to be reached and signalled they are open to it. No bought inboxes, no messages to people who never asked to hear from anyone.

The brief

Sixty seconds does two jobs at once.

The recording is both the thing we search on and the thing a candidate watches before deciding whether to answer you. That is the whole reason it is a video and not a form.

Say it

What the role needs

The problem, the two or three things that genuinely matter, and what doesn't. Sixty seconds, the way you'd say it to a colleague.

Search on it

The brief is the query

People are looked for against what you actually said you need, not a keyword string you had to guess at.

Send it

They meet you, not an ad

The invitation carries your recording. They see who is hiring and hear the role in your own words.

Answer it

Thirty seconds back

They record an answer to your brief in the app. Anyone who can't be bothered filters themselves out.

The rating

Rated against your role. Never against each other.

Every application is read against the brief you recorded and comes back as strong, partial or weak fit, with the reasoning attached.

EVIDENCE

The facts it could cite

The specific things in that person's record that answer what you asked for, written out as a list you can check.

MISSING

What it could not evidence

The requirements from your brief that nothing in their answer supports. Thin evidence is rated weak, not generous.

CLAIMS

Treated as claims

Identity is verified. What a person says about their own experience is self-reported, and the rating is written to treat it that way.

WORDS

Their own words beside it

Their video profile, transcribed, sits next to the rating — so you can check it against what the person actually said rather than take it on trust.

There is no percentage and no leaderboard. A rating is a judgement about one person against one role, and it comes with its reasons so you can disagree with it.

Straight answers

The questions recruiters actually ask.

Are you scraping people?

No. Members are reached inside FaceMeet, where they signed up to hear about roles. Outside it, for technical hires, we look at work people published themselves — GitHub, Stack Exchange, Codeforces — and someone is only approached where they have published a way to be reached and signalled they are open to it.

What does a candidate see?

Your recording. They watch the brief, then record their answer to it. They know who is hiring before they spend thirty seconds on you.

Is the score a percentage?

No. Strong, partial or weak fit, with the facts behind it and the requirements it could not evidence. A number would imply a precision that isn't there.

Do you hide the weak answers?

No. Everyone who answered is there, in order, weak ratings included. The list is not padded to look busy.

Can I run more than one role?

Yes, with a Recruiter Seat. One live role at a time is free and needs no seat at all — see pricing.

Does the candidate pay for any of this?

No. Being found, being invited, answering, and talking to you are all free on their side.

Does everyone record a fresh answer?

In the app, yes — the answer is recorded there and then, after they have watched your brief. Someone who applies from the web sends their video profile instead for now, and the dashboard tells you which of the two you are watching.

What if the role isn't technical?

The open developer sources are for technical hires, so for anything else the sourcing runs on FaceMeet's own members. We would rather say that plainly than imply we have a source for every profession.
